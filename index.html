    About

    About / Methodology

    What is a digital carbon footprint?

    Every digital action – watching a video, playing a game, asking an AI a question, saving a photo to the cloud – uses electricity in your device, the network and remote data centres. When that electricity is generated from fossil fuels, greenhouse gases are released. The total, expressed as carbon-dioxide equivalent (CO₂e), is the digital carbon footprint.

    How does DigitalCarbon work?

    1. User Input
    2. Activity Estimation
    3. Energy Estimation (kWh)
    4. Emission Factor (kg CO₂e per kWh)
    5. CO₂e Calculation
    6. Visualization
    7. Recommendations

    Estimated CO₂e (kg) = Estimated energy (kWh) × Electricity emission factor (kg CO₂e/kWh)

    Why are the results estimates?

    • Energy use per hour of streaming, gaming or per AI prompt varies widely between studies, services and devices.
    • Electricity carbon intensity changes by country, time of day and season.
    • Data-centre efficiency, network type (Wi-Fi vs mobile data) and device age all change the result.
    • The factors used here are simplified, rounded assumptions chosen for transparency, not measurements of your actual usage.
    • Device energy for streaming/gaming is already part of those activity factors, so the "Device Usage" category represents general everyday use and some overlap is possible.

    Assumptions & emission factors used

    Emission factors are informed by published research and documented assumptions, with all values centrally configured for transparency, consistency, and easy updates.

    The estimates produced by DigitalCarbon are intended for educational awareness and comparison rather than formal carbon accounting.
